# Enclosed street layout with walk grid and flow field

## Status

Accepted. Refines level-authored-as-scene-of-prefab-instances (the level is still one scene of prefab instances),
procedural-street-ground-fences-and-skyline-backdrop (the ground now follows the level's street rects instead of a fixed
grid) and world-anchored-zombie-population (slots now land only on reachable ground).

## Context

The owner found the 120 m map too open: a regular 20 m grid of 10 m streets around small 10 x 10 m blocks, so the
player could always run round a block and never felt cornered. In the reference video (see
docs/reference/original-game-video-notes.md, "Level layout") the streets are corridors: continuous building rows
with no gaps, 3.5-4 m alleys every 25-40 m, street ends closed by chain-link fences and barricades, clutter that leaves
a 4-6 m free lane, few crossings, dead ends, and zombies coming from both ends of the street and out of the alleys.

A layout like that breaks two things that worked on the open grid: the spawners placed zombies anywhere in the bounds
(inside big blocks, behind locked fences), and zombies steer straight at the player, so one behind a 40 m block presses
against its back wall. A navigation mesh would fix the second, but NavigationServer baking and agents are costly on the
single-threaded web build, and the streets are axis-aligned boxes anyway.

## Decision

- **Layout.** world/levels/freedom_city.tscn keeps the 120 x 120 m bounds but is laid out as three narrow streets:
  Main Avenue (east-west, 11 m facade to facade, 7 m road; about 5.3 character heights at 2.07 m), Back Street (9 m,
  5.5 m road, stoops; 4.3 heights) and Cross Street continuing south as the rail street (9.5 m, 6.5 m road; 4.6
  heights; a collapsed elevated rail at its dead end). That is narrower than the old 10 m grid streets the owner found
  too wide, and well under the original's 16-18 m: the owner asked for narrow streets, and parked cars on both curbs of
  Main Avenue leave a 3 m road lane plus the sidewalks. Building rows are continuous; the only ways between streets are
  four 3.5-4 m alleys, a fenced dead-end passage and the lots. Inside the blocks are small yards and bays of 10-20 m cut
  by workshops, sheds and container rows (no open plaza), the fenced basketball court (helicopter crash), a parking lot
  fenced off from Main Avenue, the gas station forecourt, a two-bay lot and a three-bay service yard. Every street end
  and two gates are chain-link fences (see-through, not walkable); the street ends carry a barricade line facade to
  facade, flush with the fence, so there is no strip to slip into. Roads narrower than 9.5 m are painted as two lanes
  (centre line only).
- **Camera side.** The camera looks from the south-east, so rows on the south and east side of a street are low
  (4-5.5 m), rows on the far side tall (7-13 m), and the building on the east side of every north-south alley, and on the
  south side of the passage, is one storey (3.5 m): a faded tall facade beside a 4 m alley still hides the player. The
  generator prints how many buildings stand between each gear spot, the start and the extraction and the camera, and
  test_level_layout requires at most one.
- **Generator.** The scene stays the source of truth; it was generated by a local, gitignored script
  (tools/level_gen/gen_freedom_city.py), which also carries the slice-17 colour grading of the environment.
- **Ground.** Ground exports `play_area`, `roads` and `lots` (Rect2 arrays) and the shader draws asphalt, lane lines,
  crosswalks and kerbs from them inside the play area; outside, the old grid continues under the skyline, and the
  streets that leave the map run on through it (CityBackdrop.open_rects keeps them free of backdrop buildings).
- **WalkGrid (rules/walk_grid.gd, node-free).** The level's layer-1 static colliders under Buildings, Fences, Props and
  Hordes (StreetNavigation.footprints_of) are drawn as boxes and circles onto a grid, grown by a clearance, and the free
  cells split into regions; the one holding the player start is "reachable". A cell is solid when its centre is within
  the grown shape, when a circle (pole, barrel) overlaps it at all, and always at a shape's centre, so a thin lamp pole
  on a cell corner is not lost. StreetNavigation builds a 1 m grid with 0.4 m clearance at level start (about 20 ms).
  PopulationGrid plans slots only on reachable cells, at the cell centre (so a zombie never starts inside a wall), and
  spreads the same map total (density per 1000 m2 of the bounds) over that ground; the director and hordes snap spawn
  spots to it.
- **FlowField (rules/flow_field.gd, node-free).** Walking distance to the player by Dial's algorithm (8 neighbours,
  no corner cutting) up to 70 m, recomputed every 0.5 s when the player has moved 1 m, GraphicsProfile
  flow_cells_per_frame cells per physics frame (600 desktop, 250 mobile). A zombie whose walk is longer than 1.15 x the
  straight line + 1.5 m follows it, with hysteresis: it returns to the straight chase only below 1.08 x + 0.25 m and
  after at least 1 s (without it, zombies froze where the whole-cell walk distance sat on the threshold). One blocked by
  a car or barrier follows the field for a few seconds; blocked again within 2.5 s it walks along the obstacle
  (wall-following, also the fallback without a field).
- **Director.** Top-up zombies spawn only where they can walk to the player within DirectorConfig.spawn_max_walk_distance
  (50 m), falling back from the 24-28 m ring to any such spot at least 80% of the ring radius away; of
  spawn_flank_candidates (4) spots it takes the one that arrives (where its walk comes within 9 m) from the side
  farthest from the living chasers (their bearings cached per flow field version), so a standing player is reached from
  both ends of the street and the alleys.
- **Tests.** tests/test_level_layout.gd rasterises the scene on a 0.5 m grid with the player's radius and checks that
  the start, every gear spot, the extraction and the hordes are reachable and that free ground is one area; it prints
  and bounds the walkable share (33%), the share of corridors under 5/8/12 m (16/45/72%), road crossings (2), open
  junctions, the ways out of a 30 m circle (mean 3.3), that world slots neither leave the reachable ground nor overlap a
  collider (physics query), the camera occlusion of the key spots and the node and light budget (under 2250 and 115).
  tests/test_surrounded.gd (deterministic: it seeds the global RNG) checks that zombies reach a standing player through
  at least two corridors within 32 s at three start-area spots, that every top-up spawn is on ground an independent
  0.5 m grid can reach, and that single zombies get round five blocks (court gate, a street corner, alleys).
  test_rules covers WalkGrid, FlowField and slot snapping on tiny maps.

## Consequences

- The map feels like the original: long narrow corridors, few escape routes, cornering at fenced ends and dead ends.
- About 33% of the map is walkable (was about 87%), so the world population is concentrated in the streets; the total
  is unchanged. Level nodes went from 2565 to about 2050 and lights from 168 to 101.
- In a 20-zombie crowd probe at five spots, all 100 zombies reached the player with the flow field; without it 75 did
  and 14 stood stuck.
- Adding a street means adding a road rect to Ground and keeping the rows closed; the layout test reports what changed.
- The grid ignores destroyed cars (it is built once); zombies then route round a wreck that is no longer there, which
  is harmless. Ground is not a @tool script, so the street markings show only when the game runs.
- The flow field cost on the web export was not measured; mobile spreads a field over more frames.
- The generator is a local tool; edits made by hand in the editor must not be overwritten by regenerating.
